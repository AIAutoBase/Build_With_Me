# Lesson 10: Capstone: build a reviewable workshop launch kit

Narrated by Clara Moretti, the AI assistant at Orbix Automation Solutions. Written by Hector Diaz. Northstar Workshops is fictional; all examples are synthetic.

## 01 · Bring the workflow together

This is Clara, the AI assistant at Orbix, narrating the last lesson of Hector's class. The capstone brings the course into one practical assignment. Northstar Workshops needs a local launch kit for a fictional creator workshop about building and reviewing useful work with Codex. Your kit contains a landing page, a synthetic CSV analysis, an evidence-backed planning brief, and a handoff that another learner can follow. The word launch describes the business purpose, not permission to publish. Keep the page local, use illustrative business details, and send no messages. A real deployment or announcement would be a separate authorized action after review. The kit should still feel complete enough to evaluate as a business deliverable. GPT-6 Astra, where available, can coordinate the reasoning across these parts. Available file, terminal, browser, and research tools perform the actual operations. You must distinguish generated content from executed checks and source-backed facts. The challenge is coherence: a polished page, accurate spreadsheet calculation, and useful brief are insufficient if they contradict one another. Your final evidence should show that the pieces agree and that their limits are visible to the next person.

## 02 · Define the four deliverables

Start with a small deliverable contract. The page should describe the fictional workshop, identify the audience, present three learning outcomes, and offer a clearly labeled local practice interaction. Reuse your earlier page rather than regenerating approved work. Keep any capacity checker supplementary unless it is already part of your design. The analysis should read a synthetic CSV, validate its fields, calculate totals and rates, and explain what the numbers cannot establish. The planning brief should use official sources for factual statements about Codex workflows and distinguish those facts from Northstar's proposed teaching choices. The handoff should identify files, preview and test commands, observed results, and unresolved decisions. Give each artifact an agreed location before asking agents to work. For example, use page files, data.csv, analysis.md, brief.md, and handoff.md inside one practice folder. These filenames are suggestions; preserve existing project conventions where applicable. Define completion as all four deliverables meeting their checks and agreeing on shared facts. Merely creating the expected filenames is not enough to satisfy the capstone.

## 03 · Validate the synthetic CSV first

Use a small synthetic dataset with four columns: workshop_id, capacity, confirmed_seats, and attended. The illustrative rows are A with twenty, sixteen, and twelve; B with twenty, twelve, and nine; and C with twenty, twenty, and fifteen. These are invented historical practice sessions, not real Northstar attendance records. Before calculating, validate the data. Workshop identifiers should be present and unique. The three numeric fields should be whole numbers. Capacity must be positive, confirmed seats must fall between zero and capacity, and attended must fall between zero and confirmed seats. Blank fields are missing values, not automatic zeros. Ask Codex to preserve the raw input and report any invalid rows rather than silently correcting them. If the supplied lab uses different synthetic rows, keep its definitions and calculate from those values; do not substitute the lesson's illustrative results. For the capstone, personal information is unnecessary. Session-level counts are enough to practice validation and analysis. This separation between raw input, validation decisions, and derived results makes the calculation reproducible and easier to review.

## 04 · Calculate and interpret the worked example

For the three illustrative rows, total capacity is sixty, confirmed seats total forty-eight, and attended totals thirty-six. Define attendance rate as attended divided by confirmed seats: thirty-six divided by forty-eight equals seventy-five percent. Define confirmation occupancy as confirmed seats divided by capacity: forty-eight divided by sixty equals eighty percent. State the numerator and denominator whenever you name a rate. These totals describe the synthetic sample only. They do not predict demand for the next workshop or prove that a landing page caused attendance. A planning observation might say the illustrative sessions contain twelve confirmed seats that did not become attendance. A causal explanation would require additional evidence that this dataset does not contain. Avoid averaging row percentages without considering denominators. In this particular sample, every row happens to have the same attendance rate, but that coincidence should not become the general method. Calculate aggregate rates from aggregate counts. If confirmed seats total zero in another dataset, attendance rate is undefined rather than zero percent. Report that case explicitly and retain the formula in your analysis.

## 05 · Build an evidence-backed planning brief

The brief answers a practical question: what should Northstar teach, and what evidence supports the factual parts of that decision? Use official documentation for claims about Codex prompting, testing, model availability, skills, or connected tools. Record the source URL and the claim it supports. Do not use documentation about a feature as evidence that the workshop will increase sales. For example, the official best-practices guide recommends providing goals, context, constraints, and completion criteria. Your teaching proposal could therefore include an exercise that rewrites a vague page request into those four parts. The documented guidance is a sourced fact; choosing that exercise is your instructional judgment. Include availability language where relevant. A model, connector, or client feature may differ by account and environment. If you cannot verify a current claim, qualify it or leave it out. The synthetic attendance analysis may inform a fictional planning discussion, but label it separately from externally sourced evidence. A strong brief makes it easy to see which conclusions rest on documentation, which on sample calculations, and which remain decisions for the workshop owner.

## 06 · Connect the page to the reviewed brief

Use the reviewed brief to check the page's promises. If the proposed workshop teaches scoped prompting, basic verification, and handoff, the learning outcomes should reflect those activities. Remove unsupported claims about guaranteed business results or universal tool access. Keep the fictional Northstar label visible and avoid inventing testimonials. Reuse the local page from lesson six where possible. Its practice interaction should remain a simulation, with no backend submission. If it uses an email field, use a synthetic address for testing and confirm that input is neither sent nor saved. If you include the supplementary capacity checker, preserve the twenty-seat contract and its validated boundaries. Test the main button, invalid input, valid practice input, narrow layout, and keyboard access using available tools or an honest manual checklist. Then compare the page with the brief and analysis. Historical synthetic session totals should not appear as real customer proof. Record any browser limitations instead of claiming unperformed checks. The integration review is complete when the page's meaning, behavior, and evidence agree with the rest of the kit.

## 07 · Write a handoff someone can reproduce

A handoff is the bridge between your work and the next person's action. Start with what was created and where it lives. Include the actual local preview procedure, any required runtime, and the commands used to validate calculations or run tests. Describe the expected visible result so a reviewer knows what to look for. Then state what you observed. If the page was inspected only in source form, say that browser behavior remains unverified. If the CSV checks passed, include the validation rules and the actual result. Link the brief's sources and identify unresolved business decisions such as a real date, pricing, or deployment destination without inventing answers. A copyable prompt is: Write a handoff for the current Northstar kit with file paths, reproduction steps, actual checks, limitations, and the next decision required. Keep expected behavior separate from observed results. Do not publish or send anything. The handoff should allow another learner to repeat a meaningful check without reconstructing your conversation. Concise instructions with evidence are more useful than a celebratory completion summary.

## 08 · Assess the kit with a clear rubric

Assess five dimensions, each from zero to two: scope control, page behavior, analysis correctness, source support, and handoff reproducibility. Zero means missing or materially incorrect. One means present but incomplete or weakly verified. Two means the requirement is met with inspectable evidence. This produces a ten-point rubric that a peer can apply consistently. A passing capstone needs at least eight points and several mandatory conditions. Synthetic data must be labeled. Calculations must be correct for the supplied rows. No unapproved publishing or messaging may occur. Completion claims must not describe checks that were never performed. A high visual score cannot compensate for fabricated evidence or incorrect arithmetic. An illustrative submission might score two for scope, one for page checks, two for analysis, two for sources, and one for handoff, totaling eight. It still needs the mandatory conditions satisfied, and its feedback should identify the missing browser check and reproduction detail. Use the rubric to direct the next correction rather than regenerating the entire kit. Reassess only the affected dimensions after the relevant fix.

## 09 · Practice days one through three

The seven-day practice plan turns the capstone into repeatable habits. On day one, take a vague Northstar request and rewrite it with a goal, context, constraints, and observable completion criteria. Spend about twenty minutes and save both versions. Your evidence is the improved brief, not the number of prompts you sent. On day two, use that brief to make one small local change, such as improving the practice confirmation or clarifying the agenda. Preserve existing work and record a visitor-journey check. Avoid expanding the exercise into a new application. On day three, investigate one real defect or supplied broken case. Record expected and actual behavior, apply a focused correction, and rerun the relevant check. The supplementary capacity lab is a suitable option: verify zero, twenty, and twenty-one requested seats with zero confirmed, then partial capacity. Finish each day with two sentences explaining what was verified and what remains uncertain. These short records build the habit of tying progress to evidence while keeping practice manageable for a working creator.

## 10 · Practice days four through seven

On day four, review one approved source using an available connected read tool or a clearly identified local export. Separate facts from inferences and make no external changes. Your evidence is a short sourced checklist and a note about the access route. On day five, analyze a small synthetic CSV, validate its rows, and show formulas and denominators. Independently check at least one total. On day six, rehearse coordination. Write two nonoverlapping ownership contracts, delegate only if supported and useful, and reconcile the outputs. You may also manually test the draft monitoring logic from lesson nine, but do not create a schedule as part of this practice plan. On day seven, ask a peer to use your handoff and score the kit with the rubric. Correct the most consequential gap and record the evidence after the correction. Keep each session to roughly twenty or thirty minutes by choosing a narrow target. This is a suggested practice sequence, not an automated reminder series. You decide when to perform each session and whether to authorize any future scheduling separately.

## 11 · Exercise: submit the complete launch kit

Complete the capstone in your own practice workspace. Read the existing guidance and reuse completed artifacts from earlier lessons. Build only what is missing, correct relevant defects, and preserve other people's edits. If the optional lab is available, use its documented setup and interfaces instead of inventing incompatible commands. Submit the local page, raw synthetic CSV, analysis, sourced brief, and handoff together. The analysis should include validation rules and formulas. The brief should identify official sources and distinguish workshop proposals from documented facts. The handoff should contain actual verification results and clear reproduction steps. Add your rubric score with a short justification for each dimension. If any mandatory condition fails, correct that issue before calling the capstone complete. If an environment limitation prevents a check, report it honestly and let the rubric reflect the missing evidence. Finally, choose one narrow improvement for the seven-day practice plan. The aim is a reviewable kit that another learner can understand and verify, not a collection of impressive descriptions or an unapproved public launch.

## 12 · Recap: a repeatable way to produce useful work

You have practiced a complete pattern for working with Codex: define the result, inspect relevant context, act through available tools, verify behavior and calculations, review the combined output, and hand it off with evidence. The pattern applies beyond workshop pages, but the Northstar example keeps each decision concrete. Remember the important boundaries. Model reasoning differs from tool execution. A skill differs from access to an external system. A draft differs from a sent message or published page. A synthetic calculation differs from a real business result. A saved monitoring prompt differs from a created schedule. Clear boundaries help you make useful progress without overstating what happened. Your capstone is complete when the four deliverables agree, the required checks are supported, and a reviewer can reproduce the essential results. The seven-day plan gives you a practical way to strengthen whichever part remains weakest. Before closing the course, explain one claim you verified, one assumption you labeled, and one action you deliberately left for a separate decision.
