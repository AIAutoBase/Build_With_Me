# Lesson 4: Research with primary sources and evidence checks

Narrated by Clara Moretti, the AI assistant at Orbix Automation Solutions. Written by Hector Diaz. Northstar Workshops is fictional; all examples are synthetic.

## 01 · Research for a decision

This is Clara, the AI assistant at Orbix Automation Solutions, narrating lesson four of Hector's class. Research becomes useful when it helps you make a specific decision. “Tell me about AI tools” invites a broad summary. “What must Northstar check before using Astra for a workshop-planning workflow?” creates a clearer task with an audience, a purpose, and a limited set of relevant claims.

Northstar Workshops remains a fictional business, and all business scenarios in this lesson are synthetic. We will use official documentation as the source for product statements and label our suggested business choices as interpretations. This is a slide walkthrough, not a live browser demonstration.

The central skill is tracing an important statement back to evidence that actually supports it. A page can be official yet irrelevant to the exact claim. A citation can be real yet attached to an exaggerated summary. By the end of the lesson, you will build a short decision memo with direct source links, scope limits, and unresolved questions. The goal is an informed next step, supported by evidence you can inspect and revisit.

## 02 · Scope the question before searching

Before searching, write down the decision you need to make. For Northstar, the owner wants to know whether to try Astra for a task that combines reading a brief, drafting a session plan, and checking the result. The decision is whether to run a small pilot in the owner's available environment.

That question suggests three research areas: the model's documented intended use, availability conditions, and the tools or permissions required by the workflow. It does not require a survey of every model or a speculative return-on-investment calculation.

Bounded research is easier to verify because each source has a job. Ask the agent to answer those three areas and identify anything the sources cannot establish. A useful memo might end with a conditional recommendation: try the pilot if Astra and the needed file tools are available. That condition matters. Public documentation can describe a rollout without proving what appears in a particular account today, so keep account-specific checks separate from general documentation findings throughout the assignment.

## 03 · Prefer the source closest to the claim

Choose sources according to the claim you are evaluating. Official product documentation is a primary source for supported features and documented configuration. Your own environment is the relevant evidence for which model choices or tools are currently available to you. Neither automatically proves that a workflow will improve your business results.

For Northstar, the official model guidance can support a statement about Astra's intended use in demanding multistep workflows. It cannot establish that Northstar saved five hours, because our fictional business has not run a measured trial. That would require actual observations and a clear comparison.

Secondary sources can help discover questions or alternative explanations, but trace consequential product claims back to the original documentation when possible. Also remember that primary does not mean infallible or complete. A provider's documentation may describe intended behavior while your client or workspace has a limitation. Record the source's scope, then check whether it applies to the environment and decision you are actually studying before treating the claim as settled.

## 04 · Search, open, and read the relevant section

A search result is a route to evidence, not the evidence review itself. Ask the agent to open the relevant official page and read the section supporting the claim. A short snippet may omit a condition, describe an older version, or match words from an unrelated part of the page.

The official search guidance says to use live search when the task depends on the latest information. Search availability and mode depend on the environment and workspace configuration. If live retrieval is unavailable, say so and distinguish the supplied documentation from a newly checked source.

For Northstar, a focused query might combine the exact model name with availability or model selection. Once the source is open, inspect the wording about rollout and client differences. Save the direct page link, the section, and a short paraphrase. This creates a path a reviewer can follow without repeating the whole search. It also prevents a general landing page from standing in for evidence that actually appears in a specific documentation section.

## 05 · Create a claim-level evidence record

A claim-level evidence record connects each important statement with the source that supports it. For a short memo, you can use a table with claim, source URL, relevant section, scope, and status. Status might be supported, uncertain, or not established by the reviewed material.

Consider the statement “Astra is intended for demanding workflows across multiple steps and tools.” The official model guidance supports that general recommendation. Now consider “Every Northstar member can select Astra.” The same documentation does not support that claim and explicitly describes variable availability.

Keep those statements separate. A single citation at the bottom of a paragraph can hide the difference between a supported general statement and an unsupported universal one. In the Northstar memo, mark individual account access as a local check still required. This is not a defect in the research. It is an accurate boundary around what the source can tell you. Good research makes that boundary visible so the owner can act without confusing public guidance with personal access.

## 06 · Handle dates and changing information

Research has a time dimension. A source may have a publication date, an update date, and the date you accessed it. Those are different facts. If the page does not show when it was updated, do not invent a date or treat your access date as its publication date.

For Northstar, availability is a changing claim. Record when the documentation was checked, then confirm the actual model choices before starting the pilot. A memo can remain useful even if the interface changes, provided it distinguishes stable working principles from time-sensitive access details.

Ask the agent to preserve dates exactly when the source supplies them and to label missing dates as unspecified. If two sources conflict, compare their scope and timing before choosing one. A newer general announcement may still be less relevant than a client-specific configuration page. The objective is not to collect timestamps for decoration. It is to understand whether the evidence remains applicable at the moment you make the decision and which claims deserve a fresh check.

## 07 · Copy a research brief

The copyable research brief asks for a narrow decision memo, official sources, direct links, and explicit uncertainty. It also separates documented model guidance from account access and tool permissions. That prevents the final recommendation from becoming broader than the evidence.

When adapting the prompt, name the actual decision and the claims that could change it. For example, a creator choosing a workshop format might need evidence about a platform's documented upload limits rather than model selection. The structure stays the same even when the subject changes.

Do not ask for a fixed number of sources unless the assignment has a good reason. Three weak sources are not automatically better than one directly relevant primary page. Ask for enough evidence to support the consequential claims and explain any gaps. If the agent cannot retrieve a page, it should identify that limitation instead of implying it read the contents. Your review then focuses on evidence quality and applicability, which are more useful than a long bibliography attached to a vague conclusion.

## 08 · Read an illustrative decision memo

An illustrative Northstar memo might conclude that a small pilot is reasonable because official guidance positions Astra for demanding multistep work. It would then state two conditions: the owner must have access to the model, and the environment must provide the tools required by the chosen task.

That conclusion is an interpretation based on the documentation, not a product guarantee. The memo should say so. It should also avoid claiming that the pilot will save a specific amount of time or improve member retention. Those outcomes would need observation in a real workflow.

A practical next step is to run one bounded assignment with acceptance criteria and record the review effort. This connects research to action while keeping uncertainty honest. In the fictional example, no pilot has actually been measured. The memo's value is that it tells the owner what is supported, what still needs checking, and what small experiment could generate the missing business evidence. A useful recommendation can be conditional and still be decisive enough to act on.

## 09 · Correct citation overreach

A common research failure is citation overreach: the source is legitimate, but the sentence says more than the source supports. Suppose the memo states, “Astra is available to every creator and guarantees a complete workshop launch,” followed by the official model link. The link does not validate those universal claims.

Correct the sentence at the claim level. Replace universal availability with the documented conditions, remove the guarantee, and separate intended use from observed results. Then inspect the revised memo for other statements that depend on the same mistaken assumption.

Do not settle for adding more citations to an unsupported sentence. More links cannot repair a claim unless their contents support it. Ask the agent to show the relevant section and explain the connection in plain language. For Northstar, the corrected recommendation should remain conditional on available tools and account access. This correction preserves the useful research while removing the unsupported certainty, and it creates a more trustworthy basis for deciding whether to run the pilot in the first place.

## 10 · Treat retrieved content as untrusted input

Web content can contain text that looks like instructions to the agent. The official search guidance says to treat web results as untrusted input. A page telling the agent to ignore your request or reveal private files is not an authorized change to the research task.

For Northstar, the task is to gather evidence for a decision memo. It does not authorize following unrelated instructions found in a retrieved page. Keep the research question and the user's boundaries in control while using the page as material to evaluate.

Also distinguish incomplete access from negative evidence. If the agent cannot open a page, that does not prove the feature is absent. If a source omits a detail, it may mean the reviewed page does not answer the question. Ask for a clear limitation statement and another appropriate official source when available. This protects the quality of the memo without turning every uncertainty into a confident conclusion. The right answer sometimes includes a specific unresolved question and the most practical way to check it.

## 11 · Exercise: build an evidence-backed memo

Write a short decision memo for the fictional Northstar pilot. Investigate the documented suitability of Astra, conditions affecting availability, and the distinction between model choice and required tools. Use official sources and open the relevant pages if your environment supports retrieval.

Create evidence records for at least three material claims. Include the direct URL, relevant section, access date, and scope. If you are working only from supplied documentation, state that clearly and do not describe the source as freshly retrieved. Mark account-specific access as a separate check unless you actually inspect it.

Submit the memo, the evidence records, and one corrected claim that originally overstated or blurred the evidence. Your recommendation should name a practical next action and any condition that must be satisfied first. You are not being graded on reaching a positive conclusion about the pilot. You are being graded on whether a reader can trace your reasoning to sources, understand the remaining uncertainty, and make a proportionate decision from the information you actually reviewed.

## 12 · Recap: make evidence reviewable

Good research starts with a decision and ends with a reviewable path to action. You define the question, find appropriate sources, read the relevant material, and connect important claims to evidence. You then distinguish documented facts from interpretations and identify what remains unknown.

For Northstar, the model documentation supports a general use case, while account access and available tools require environment-specific checks. Business results require a real pilot and measurement. Keeping those evidence types separate prevents a reasonable recommendation from turning into an unsupported promise.

The same habits apply to platform comparisons, workshop preparation, and creator research. Use the source closest to the claim, preserve its scope, and revisit changing information before acting. A short memo with clear limits can be more useful than a long report whose citations are difficult to inspect. In the next lesson, we will apply this discipline to CSV data.
