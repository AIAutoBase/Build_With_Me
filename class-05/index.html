This class fails differently, and that changes everything about it

Every other class in this series fails loudly. A webhook 404s. A voice note comes back as static. A graph renders with placeholder names. You find out immediately.

A digest that stops firing sends you nothing. And nothing looks exactly like a quiet Tuesday.

You will not notice for a week. By then you will have made decisions on the assumption that nothing came in, and you will be wrong.
So we build it to always send

Even when the answer is "nothing came in overnight."

That sounds like noise and it is not. A digest that only speaks when it has news is indistinguishable from a broken one. The empty digest is the heartbeat.

It is the single most important design decision in the hour, and the one people talk themselves out of.

It also changes what "verify" means here. The question is never "did it work?" — it is "how would I know if it stopped?"

Before the hour · 10 minutes

1
The probe that decides half the class

Can your mail server accept an IMAP APPEND? That is the command that puts a message into a folder — the difference between reading your mail and writing a draft into it.

the pack probes it properly
bash verify.sh

It connects, finds your drafts folder, appends a throwaway with the \Draft flag, confirms it landed, and deletes it again.

Why a probe and not a support article

"My provider supports IMAP" tells you nothing about whether APPEND works on your account, into that folder, with that flag. Three things have to be true at once and only one of them is on any feature page.

If your check for "can I write a draft?" is reading documentation, you have not checked anything.

2
Find out what your Drafts folder is really called

There is no universal name, and this costs people twenty minutes.

ServerUsually
Many IMAP serversDrafts
Courier / some cPanel hostsINBOX.Drafts
Gmail over IMAP[Gmail]/Drafts
Localised serversBorradores, Entwürfe…
Get it wrong and your drafts become invisible

Write to a name your server does not use and IMAP happily creates that folder. Your drafts are real, they are being written, and your mail client never shows you the folder they are in.

3
Check the timezone
on the brain machine
docker compose exec -T n8n printenv GENERIC_TIMEZONE
date
A 7am schedule on a UTC container fires at 2am

And nothing about that looks broken. The workflow runs, the email sends, the logs are green — it just happens while you are asleep. n8n reads environment variables at container start and never again, so changing it needs a restart.

4
Decide what you actually want at 7am

Not technical, and the one that decides whether you keep using this.

  • What would make you glad it arrived? "Three new leads, one invoice, one thing that needs a reply today."
  • What would make you start ignoring it? "Forty-one items processed."
  • What time do you actually read email? If it is 9am, do not send it at 7am.
A digest you delete unread is worse than no digest

Because you are training yourself to ignore something your own brain sends you. Bring a real answer — we build it around yours.

The build — two prompts

P1 alone is a complete, useful thing. If P2 fights you, you still have a working morning digest and you have not lost the hour.

P1
The clock

A schedule trigger, a query over last night, one email.

Build the query first, on its own

Before any schedule and any email. Getting SQL wrong inside a scheduled workflow is miserable, because it only runs once a day and you cannot see it.

Counts come from SQL. The model never counts.

Models are bad at counting and confidently wrong about it. Pass it the numbers and let it write prose around them — never ask it to tally.

Milestone

An email in your inbox. Read it out loud. Is it something you would want at 7am, or noise with a timestamp?

Why not just send the reply?

★
The argument, said out loud
0:30 – 0:36

The first thing your brain does to the world rather than for you should not be able to embarrass you.

Not because the model writes badly — it writes fine. Because you have not earned the right to trust it yet, and neither has it.

Draft-don't-send is how you find out whether it would have been right, at zero risk, for as long as you need.

Some people will move to auto-send after a month of good drafts. That is a decision to make with evidence — a hundred drafts you have actually read, on your own account, in a narrow category rather than everything.

It is not a default, and this pack will not make it one for you

There is no send node in P2 and there should not be. If you add one later, add it on purpose.

P2 — the hands

P2
Write it, append it, stop
0:36 – 0:50
Two things that cost the most time here

No \Draft flag → the message appears as received mail. It sits in the folder looking like something a person sent you, and some clients will not even offer to edit it. The most confusing five minutes in the class.

No In-Reply-To → the draft does not thread. It is a new email that happens to start with "Re:", which looks almost right — and almost right is worse than wrong, because you will not question it.

An APPEND takes a real message, not a body

A complete RFC 5322 blob: From:, To:, Subject:, Date:, In-Reply-To:, then a blank line, then the body. A missing Date: makes some clients show today, some 1970, and some nothing at all.

Cap how many it writes per run

A loop with no limit meets a busy night and writes ninety drafts. You will delete all of them without reading one, and then you will stop trusting the whole thing.

Milestone — in a real mail client, not in n8n

A draft your brain wrote, in Drafts, threaded under the original, editable, unsent.

Then the question that matters: would you have sent it as written?

The feedback loop is the actual product

The workflow takes an hour. Getting the drafts good takes weeks. Read ten, write down what is wrong — tone, length, invented facts, or that it should not have replied at all — and feed that back into the prompt.

The closing beat — break it on purpose

★
Watch nothing happen
0:50 – 0:55

Deactivate the workflow. Look at your inbox.

From where you sit, a dead workflow and a quiet morning are identical.

That is the thing you are actually guarding against — and it is why the empty digest is not optional.

Then turn it back on. You want to have seen it, not just been told.
