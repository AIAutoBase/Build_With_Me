No call has been placed. No latency has been measured. No number has been bought.

The pack ships with DRAFT in its filename and that is not decoration. The five-stage latency budget you will see is a budget written by somebody who has not yet made the call. Build prompt 14 instruments all five stages and the verification list fills them in. The DRAFT comes off when it does.

There is precedent in this series: Class 4 shipped a DRAFT zip for exactly this reason, and the name changed when the measurement existed.

Clara says she is an AI, and it is a file

Not a system-prompt instruction. A recorded audio file, played before the model receives a single token.

A prompt instruction is a request. A caller asking *"wait, am I talking to a real person?"* warmly enough has talked models out of firmer instructions than this one — and it does not take an adversary. An agreeable model deciding that reassurance is the helpful answer gets there on its own.

A file cannot be talked out of anything, because by the time the model is involved the sentence has already been said.

The disclosure has to land inside the first four seconds, because that is roughly how long a caller waits before starting to talk, and a disclosure the caller talked over is a disclosure that did not happen. The renderer refuses to build a greeting that buries it.

Twilio, not a turnkey voice platform

The obvious choice would have been a service that gives you a voice agent on a phone number in an afternoon. We are not using one.

Turnkey platformTwilio plus our own pipeline
Time to first callAn afternoonA class
What you learnTheir consoleWhere every millisecond goes
VoiceTheirsYours
When it breaksA support ticketA number in a table you can read

Eleven weeks of this series have been about owning the pieces. A class that teaches a vendor's dashboard teaches something that expires.

The honest cost: more parts, and latency becomes our problem instead of theirs. That is a real trade and it gets said in the first five minutes rather than discovered at minute forty.

The latency budget — and it is a budget

Target: under 1.2 seconds from the caller finishing a sentence to the first audio coming back. Past about 1.5 s a caller starts talking again. Past 2 s the call feels broken.

StageBudgetNote
Media stream in~100 msNot ours to improve
Endpointing — deciding they stopped~200 msOurs, and the easiest place to lose half a second
Speech to text~300 msStreaming, or this figure is fiction
Model, to first token~400 msIncludes the zone-scoped memory query
Text to speech, to first chunk~200 msStreaming, or this figure is fiction

Every one of these is unmeasured. Non-streaming speech-to-text plus non-streaming text-to-speech is four to six seconds per turn — not a slower version of this system, a different and unusable one.

A phone caller reaches two of your four zones

general and business. Never personal. Never clients.

Whoever is on the phone is unauthenticated. Caller ID is not authentication: it is trivially spoofed, and when honest it identifies a handset rather than a person.

That list is a hardcoded constant in the source. Not a setting, not an environment variable, not a dashboard control — because a configurable value has a default, and somebody widens it at 2am to test something and it stays that way. Nothing ever reports a problem, because from every direction except a caller asking the right question, a widened scope behaves identically to a correct one.

This is what Class 10 was built for. And when Clara cannot answer, she says something true — *that one's in a part of the brain this number doesn't reach* — without ever having searched the zones she cannot reach. The restricted documents were never loaded into the process, so there is nothing there to leak under any prompt.

Clara takes messages. She does not commit

She can say what is on the calendar. She cannot book. She can take a callback request. She cannot promise a price, a date, a discount or a scope.

The rule is in the system prompt and in a filter on the way out. Prompt rules drift under conversational pressure; an output filter does not. When it fires it does not edit the sentence — editing a commitment out of a sentence leaves a sentence that sounds like a commitment with a word missing. It replaces the whole reply.

Twenty phrases in English and Spanish that must be caught, five ordinary answers that must not be. The five matter more: a filter that catches everything is a system that says nothing.

The five traps

TrapWhy it costs time
1No barge-inThe caller interrupts, Clara talks over them for eleven seconds. Nothing errors. In testing you politely wait for her to finish, so it never happens once
2The tunnel makes the box reachableSignature validation is the lock, and it is one line that is easy to leave for later
3Speech-to-text hallucinates on a bad lineSilence and line noise produce confident, grammatical sentences that were never said. The brain then answers them
4The number costs money whether it rings or notMonthly rental plus per minute. Say the figure out loud
5A GPU busy with something elseLocal text-to-speech only. Latency triples, intermittently, with no error in any log

What you can verify before class

node check-call-scope.mjs

Seven checks. Four about the handler: the zone list is a hardcoded constant; it is exactly general and business; every search call site passes zones — checked in the source *and* by running a turn with a spy; and the commitment filter runs after the model, not before. Three about the filter and its twenty-five test phrases.

It places no call and sends no message.

What this does not protect against

  1. A determined caller with a convincing story. The filter catches phrasings. It does not

catch a caller who talks Clara into revealing something she was allowed to know.

  1. The tunnel. It is a door into the box holding eleven weeks of work, and it is open for

as long as you leave it open.

  1. Two people calling at once. Undecided, and unbuilt.
